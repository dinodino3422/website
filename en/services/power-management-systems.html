Power Management Systems (PMS)

The PMS is the system that decides, without asking anyone, how many generators run and what gets dropped when demand exceeds available power. Set up correctly, the crew never notices it. Set up badly, they notice it as a blackout.

We take on the study, the setup and the verification of the whole power-management logic — from load-dependent start through to the restart sequence after a blackout, proven by real testing rather than simulation alone.

Scope of work

  • Power-balance study and load-dependent start / stop thresholds
  • Generator priority and running-hours rotation setup
  • Heavy-consumer request logic for bow thrusters, cargo pumps, compressors, deck machinery
  • Staged load shedding — group definition and trip timing
  • Blackout detection and automatic restart sequence, including emergency-generator handover
  • PMS interface to the alarm monitoring system and event recorder
  • Acceptance testing against real load or a load bank

Typical symptoms & failures

  • Blackout on a sudden demand step — bow thruster or a large motor starting
  • Generators hunting: starting and stopping repeatedly around the same load point
  • Load shedding that never triggers, or drops the wrong consumer group
  • No automatic recovery after a blackout; the vessel needs a manual procedure
  • The emergency generator takes over and will not release when main power returns

What you receive

  • Documented power balance and threshold table
  • Load-shedding group table with order and timing
  • Blackout-recovery test protocol
  • A one-page operating note for bridge and engine room

Equipment & protocols

  • DEIF-based and PLC-based PMS platforms
  • Integration with ATS, tie breakers and shore connection
  • Load banks for acceptance testing
  • Operation aligned with SOLAS II-1 requirements for electrical power and restoration

Frequently asked questions

Does the vessel have to be taken out of service for testing?

Blackout-recovery tests need a controlled interruption and are scheduled with the vessel in a safe condition — usually at anchor or in dock, fully coordinated with the technical department.

Can a PMS be added to an older vessel without replacing the switchboard?

Often yes. If the breakers have motorised operation and CT/VT are present, the PMS goes in as a control upgrade with the switchboard left in place.

How often should PMS settings be reviewed?

After every change to the vessel's electrical load — new machinery, a retrofit, a change of trade. A 2015 power balance does not describe the 2026 vessel.
