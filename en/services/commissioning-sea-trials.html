Commissioning & Sea Trials

Commissioning is the only moment a system is tested systematically in every state it can enter — and it is the moment most often compressed because the vessel has to sail.

We work to written protocols per system and per scenario, with measurements recorded and signed. A project is not complete because it works, but because it has been proven to work under fault conditions.

Scope of work

  • Commissioning protocols drafted per system, before work begins
  • Loop checking and verification of every I/O from field to screen
  • Functional testing of control logic, interlocks and safety shutdowns
  • Paralleling, load-sharing and load-rejection testing
  • Blackout and automatic recovery testing
  • Emergency generator, ATS and shore-connection testing
  • Attendance at sea trials and class surveys
  • Handover of a complete test file

Typical symptoms & failures

  • A project handed over with no test protocols
  • A system that works in normal operation but has never been tested in fault
  • Class observations due to incomplete documentation
  • Recurring problems immediately after a job is signed off

What you receive

  • Signed protocols per system and per test scenario
  • Recorded measurements and controller screenshots
  • A prioritised punch list
  • A handover file ready for survey

Equipment & protocols

  • Load banks, primary/secondary injection sets, recorders
  • Power-quality analysers and precision portable instruments
  • Tools for extracting event logs from controllers and AMS

Frequently asked questions

Can you commission work carried out by someone else?

Yes, and it is common. We test independently and record what we find, with no interest in covering for anyone.

Do you attend tests witnessed by class?

Yes. We prepare the system and the documentation and attend the tests.

How long does full PMS commissioning take?

For a two or three gen-set installation, typically 2–4 days including blackout testing, provided the installation is ready.
